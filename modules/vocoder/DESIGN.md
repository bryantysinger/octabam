# VOCODER: design note

The knobs and the measured figures are in [`README.md`](README.md); this note keeps the reasons.

## The model: the Roland VP-330

From the VP-330 service notes (Roland, 21 Sept 1979, on archive.org as `ROLAND_VP330_service_notes`), checked in the text: "ten BPFs with a high Q" covering 170 Hz to 7 kHz, at 200, 280, 400, 600, 900, 1.3k, 2k, 2.8k, 4k and 6k Hz (the FLH16 table); the microphone compressed and pre-emphasised before analysis; a synthesis response "equal to de-emphasized characteristics"; a "High Consonant Circuit" that passes the voice's top end to the output. Read from the schematic, with moderate confidence (a low-resolution scan): each band is two 2nd-order band-passes tuned apart, on both the analysis and the synthesis side; peak-detector envelopes with about 10 ms of release; the consonant high-pass is 6th order near 4 kHz; no noise generator in the vocoder.

## Why the bands are steep

The first version used sixteen 2nd-order bands. It sounded like a vocoder but the words were lost: a 2nd-order band is only about 6 dB down at its neighbour, so each envelope averages over two octaves and the formants smear, while the long-term spectrum still looks right. The VP-330's pairs are about 18 dB down at the neighbour. On the same voice (a public-domain LibriVox reading) the VP-330 law scored 0.816 on STOI against 0.764 for the same design with 2nd-order bands, and the tester heard the steep version as "clearly better" and "very intelligible". The vocoder literature agrees: spectral contrast matters more than the number of bands (Shannon et al. 1995; Xu, Thompson and Pfingst 2005; Fu and Nogaki 2005).

## The 24-bit version, and what changed

- Each band section is a Chamberlin state-variable filter with its input scaled by q = 1/7, so the section peaks at its input's level and no state outgrows the 24-bit range. Two words of state a section; four sections a band (two on the voice, two on the carrier).
- The pre-emphasis is folded into each band's output weight, with the de-emphasis (9 dB across the bands) and the pair's gain at its centre. Ahead of the filters it cut the 200 Hz band by about 20 dB, which left its rounding 51 dB under the signal; folded, the law matches the float model within 0.9996 correlation.
- The envelope followers hold 48 bits. In 24 their release step fell below one unit near zero, so they stuck at about -90 dBFS, and with the make-up gain the carrier leaked into the pauses.
- The envelope is a peak detector, e = max(v, e (1 - 0.00227)): instant attack and a 10 ms release, the VP-330's diodes. (The first version had a 0.3 ms attack; the detector is cheaper and closer to the original.)
- The band sum is 24 bits, rounded: with 48 it cost two moves a band, and four instances did not fit a core. Its rounding sits about 62 dB under the vocoded signal and is zero in a pause.
- The VP-330's extra 2.2 ms envelope smoother is left out: with it, ten bands would need 120 of the 132 state words. Without it the output differs by about 19 dB under the signal, slightly buzzier; STOI was unchanged (0.828).
- The built-in carrier is the VP-330's 8' and 4' ramps: two polyBLEP sawtooths on one phase, the 4' being the phase doubled. The polyBLEP correction is branch-free: (1 - min(u/du, 1))^2 - (1 - min((1 - u)/du, 1))^2, with 1/du from a table per NOTE. At C6 the aliasing in the bands is 61 dB under the harmonics.

## 0.2: the performance controls

Chosen by ear from float prototypes (`renders6`, 5 Oct 2026): BEND, GEND, WHSP and GLIDE in; a band-shifting formant control (FORM: uninteresting), an envelope freeze (HOLD: better tape-freeze effects exist) and a chord carrier (an external carrier does it) out.

- **Page 1 is the performance surface.** Scenes and the crossfader morph page-1 slots only (stock cannot scene-lock page 2), so the continuous controls take page 1 and LEVL left (the track's AMP sets level).
- **BEND and GLIDE: one log pitch.** P = NOTE + 12 + 24 (BEND - 1/2) semitones above C0 (with SCAL, KEY and RANG, below: q(NOTE) + 12 + 2 B (BEND - 1/2), B the reach), glided per call in 48 bits (P_s += n7 K (P - P_s), so the time constant does not depend on how the host splits a block). The increment is OCT[o] 2^r/2 from x = 4/3 P_s (o = floor(8x), r = frac(8x)), 2^r a quintic within 0.0002 cents, and 1/du is one 48-by-24 division per call. Every step truncates: rounding modes are a hardware detail, and truncation lets `vocoder_ref.py` reproduce the increment exactly, which the sample-by-sample gate needs (one LSB of increment drifts a 7 kHz harmonic by a sixth of a cycle in a second). The polyBLEP's scale went from 2^11 to 2^12 so that C0 (C1 bent down an octave) fits a Q23 word.
- **GEND: formant scaling, not band shifting.** Female formants sit about 15-20 % above male ones. Shifting the voice's envelopes by whole bands (half an octave each) was coarse and odd; retuning the analysis pairs to f x A while the synthesis pairs stay put scales every formant by 1/A, smoothly. With y = GEND - 1/2 (-1/2 .. +1/2), the formants move by 2^t octaves, t = y above the centre and y/2 below: x0.84 (-3 semitones) at -64, x1.41 (+6) at +63. The first law was symmetric, +-3 semitones (A = 2^(-y/2)); on the unit (OCTABAM13, 5 Oct 2026) it was too subtle, and of +-3, +-6 and +-9 rendered by the float model Ignorato chose -3 for the male side and +6 for the female. A = 2^-t by a cubic over [-1/4, 1/2); at the centre it is exactly 1, so 0.1's tuning is unchanged. The analysis weight stays the band's own, so the female side is slightly brighter, the male side slightly darker. The top pair is limited at the largest Q23 coefficient (about 7.4 kHz) at the male end.
- **Where GEND's coefficients live.** The state block was full (127 of 132 words), and the table is shared by every instance on the core, so the 20 analysis coefficients go in the instance's allocator slot, stock DELAY's mechanism (`Claims.stock_instance_buffer`, 20 words): base read at init through `x:>$213`, written every call, read by the band loop with `y:(r3)+` where 0.1 read the table. VOCODER runs only at FX2 positions 1 and 3 (T2, T4, T6, T8 since 11 Oct 2026; positions 1 and 2 before), so the slot is Y:0x8000 (position 1) or position 3's buffer in the shared window (0x34000 on core 0, 0x3c000 on core 1), 256 words in (a probe at 0x30000 itself buzzed), so the 20 words are Y:0x8100, 0x34100 or 0x3c100; the claim reserves the core's half (0x30000-0x37FFF, 0x38000-0x3FFFF) for the ledger. Nothing else in the remix writes near them (emulator and ledger, 11 Oct 2026): stock's core 1 to core 0 mailbox is at 0x37F00 (inside core 0's position-3 slot, 0x3E00 words above VOCODER's), the bus scratch at 0x36000 exists only in a bus remix, and a stock effect writes its own slot only, which on that track is VOCODER's. GEND's coefficients are rewritten only on a call where GEND (smoothed, below) moved.
- **WHSP:** a 24-bit LCG (x' = 1664525 x + 1013904223 mod 2^24, a 6.3 s period, fine under ten band envelopes), scaled to the sawtooth mix's RMS (G = 0.316) and blended: c + w (G noise - c). About 15 instructions a sample.
- **The de-zipper.** A scene jump, a fast crossfader or a p-lock reaches the DSP as a new knob word on one call. GEND, WHSP, DRY and CONS each follow their knob by s += n7 K (T - s) once a call, rounded (`macr`), K for 8 ms, the time constant GLDE OFF gives NOTE and BEND; so every page-1 control lands over a few ms. Rounded, a settled value stands still (truncation would creep down by one LSB a call, and GEND would rewrite its coefficients for each). No r7 slot was free: WHSP, DRY and CONS keep their smoothed values in the slots that held the block's knob values, GEND in the word that held the last value written to the Y slot, whose -1 after init makes the first call take the knob as it is (the others start from 0 and reach their knob within a few ms of a load). `vocoder_law.dz_step` models it bit for bit.
- **SCAL, KEY and RANG: the pitch's page-2 selectors** (18 Oct 2026). They sit on page 2's blank slots 7, 10 and 9, so value 0 of each must be 0.2 as it was: a part saved before them holds 0 there. SCAL is a lookup, not a search: DOWN[scale][d], the semitones from degree d above KEY down to the scale note at or below it (0 to 2 in every scale here; CHRO's row all 0), with d = (n + 12 - KEY) mod 12 by three conditional subtractions (48, 24, 12). The snapped note q(n) = n - DOWN is the largest scale note not above n, so q never decreases as n rises: a sweep never goes backwards, and every scale note is the knob value that names it. "At or below" rather than "nearest" keeps the rule one table read and lets a p-lock of a scale note play that note; nearest would need a tie rule and could jump up a semitone ahead of the knob. RANG scales BEND by R = 3/16, 3/32 or 3/8 from a three-word table, so value 0 is the same multiply 0.2 made. Then P is held to [0, 96 x 2^16): C0, below which 1/(du 2^12) no longer fits its Q23 word (the 0.2 law reached C0 exactly, at C1 with BEND -64), and just under C8, past which OCT has no octave (the natural top, C6 + 23.6 semitones, is under it). All of it is per call, on the knob words, before the glide, so GLDE glides between snapped notes and the per-sample loop is unchanged; `vocoder_law.pitch_target` is the same arithmetic in Python, and `verify_vocoder` holds the DSP's increment to it on every call of every scale and key. The tables add 123 words to the P table (166 in all, in the stock curve bank).
- **CHRD (an extra carrier voice) is out of 0.2.** A voice is two more polyBLEP sawtooths, about 60-90 instructions a sample; two VOCODERs a core plus four FX1 FILTERs already leave a few hundred cycles of the 3,120 with the real cost uncertain by about 20 % (CHIP.md section 2). It needs a cycle measurement on the unit first; an external carrier on input B (MODE EXT) plays chords meanwhile.

## r7 slots and tables

The header of `vocoder.asm` has the slot map (ten blocks of ten words, the carrier phase, the consonant filter, per-sample and per-block scratch: 127 of the 132) and the P table (each band's coefficients in the order the loop reads them, OCT, GLD, RANG's R and the scales' DOWN rows: 166 words). The band loop walks its states with (r5)+ so the filter arithmetic carries its loads and stores as parallel moves; scratch sits low so its moves take one word. `vocoder_law.py` computes the tables for the manifest; `vocoder_ref.py` uses the same functions, so the two cannot disagree.

## What would falsify it

- The DSP differing from `vocoder_ref.py` by more than -50 dB, or in level by more than 0.1 dB.
- Carrier audible in a pause: above -100 dBFS 0.3 s after the voice stops.
- A centre sine leaking into a neighbouring band less than 15 dB down.
